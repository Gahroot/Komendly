Businesses should switch selected text reviews to video testimonials when a spoken customer story is worth testing at a buying decision. My recommendation is to keep the written review alongside the video, preserve its meaning, and measure completed purchases or qualified leads. Komendly turns text reviews into AI-avatar videos.

The useful question is whether a particular customer story deserves another format. Start with the objection you need to answer, then decide how to present the evidence. A broad video marketing statistic should not make that decision for you.

## What do the numbers actually say about video testimonials?

Wyzowl’s 2026 report surveyed 266 unique respondents in late 2025, including marketing professionals and online consumers.[^1] It reports that 91% of businesses use video as a marketing tool.[^1] Separately, testimonial videos appear among the formats used by 57% of video marketers, who could select multiple video types.[^1]

Those findings concern adoption and format use. They should not be interpreted as evidence that businesses are replacing written reviews, or that changing formats produces a particular conversion increase.

My recommendation: use the research as a reason to consider a test. Build your business case from your own results rather than borrowing a performance promise from an adoption survey.

## Why choose video testimonials instead of text reviews?

I would choose video when I want a customer story presented aloud, with deliberate pacing and a clear narrative. I would keep text available for visitors who want to read the original wording without playing anything.

For the first experiment, choose a story with a beginning, a decision, and an outcome. Ask whether each part helps answer the buyer’s question. Remove introductions and repetition before adding music or visual effects.

Keep the distinction between delivery and evidence clear. A recorded interview presents the customer speaking. For AI-avatar narration, label the presentation explicitly rather than suggesting the customer appeared on camera.

If your starting point is a written review, the related guide on [turning customer reviews into video testimonials without a camera](/blog/video-testimonials-without-camera-ai-solution) covers that production route.

## What is the cost of keeping text-only reviews?

I would not assign a revenue loss to text-only reviews without a controlled test. Instead, write a test brief before commissioning or generating videos.

Record the page you want to improve, the customer objection you want to address, and the outcome that would justify the work. Include production time, review time, editing, and placement in your cost estimate.

For an online store, I would prioritize completed purchases. For a service business, I would prioritize qualified enquiries or booked consultations. Choose the outcome before looking at results.

Avoid changing the offer, headline, page layout, and testimonial format together. My recommendation is to keep the experiment focused so the result is easier to interpret. Treat an inconclusive result as a reason to investigate, rather than a reason to claim success.

## Which customer stories should different businesses test?

Use these as editorial starting points, not industry performance benchmarks.

### Real estate

I would ask a client to explain what they were uncertain about, how the agent helped, and which part of the process mattered most. Keep the story focused on that client’s experience rather than making promises about another property transaction.

### Restaurants and local services

I would select a review describing a particular visit or service interaction. Preserve what happened and why it mattered. Avoid rewriting a modest compliment into an exaggerated recommendation.

### SaaS and professional services

I would choose a story about a specific workflow, implementation decision, or support experience. If it includes a measured outcome, keep the customer’s context attached rather than turning it into a general product promise.

Keep the source review, seek the customer’s permission for the planned use, and review the final presentation against the original. Those are editorial safeguards I recommend, rather than a claim that a particular workflow guarantees legal compliance.

FTC guidance says reviews featured in advertising or marketing become testimonials; the exemption for mere review hosting does not apply.[^2] It also explains that AI avatars are not blanket-banned, but fake or false underlying testimonials can violate the rule, and avatar use can be deceptive under the FTC Act.[^2]

For an avatar presentation, I recommend a visible label such as “AI-avatar narration of a customer’s written review.” Do not treat that label as a substitute for reviewing the underlying content and overall impression.

## How can you prepare a video testimonial worth testing?

Use my **PROOF checklist** before producing the asset. This is a practical editorial framework, not a validated performance score.

- **Permission:** Confirm the customer agrees to the proposed wording, attribution, format, and placement.
- **Relevance:** Match the story to a buying question on the destination page.
- **Original meaning:** Compare the script with the source review. Preserve qualifications and context.
- **Open presentation:** Identify avatar narration or other presentation choices that viewers might misunderstand.
- **Focused measurement:** Select the business outcome and evaluation approach before publishing.

If an item is unresolved, fix it before increasing production. A polished presentation is not a reason to skip the source check.

### Prepare the review

Save the original wording and your proposed edit together. Mark anything removed or paraphrased, then ask the customer to approve the presentation you intend to publish.

### Prepare the viewing experience

Include captions and check them against the final audio. W3C explains that captions make audio information accessible to people who are deaf or hard of hearing; they include dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio.[^3]

### Prepare the experiment

Keep the original written review available. Test adding the video in a defined location, and record which version each visitor receives. Decide in advance how you will handle incomplete or inconclusive results.

## What should your first video testimonial action plan include?

Start small enough that you can inspect every decision.

First, audit your existing reviews. Choose a genuine story that answers a specific objection. If you cannot identify that objection, reconsider the selection before producing anything.

Next, choose the destination. I would start with a relevant product or service page rather than placing the same testimonial everywhere. Write down why that customer’s experience belongs beside that offer.

Then create the presentation. Keep the approved wording close at hand while editing. Check attribution, captions, and any avatar label on the actual page, including its mobile layout.

Finally, evaluate the chosen business outcome alongside the effort required to maintain the asset. Video plays can be useful diagnostic information, but my recommendation is to make the expansion decision around purchases or qualified leads.

If the story itself feels vague, revisit [why your five-star reviews aren’t converting](/blog/why-your-5-star-reviews-arent-converting) before producing more versions.

## What questions should you ask before switching?

### Should you remove the written review?

I recommend keeping it available alongside the video so the experiment adds a presentation option without discarding the source wording.

### How long should the video be?

Include enough context to make the experience understandable, then cut repetition. Treat duration as an editorial choice to test rather than a universal benchmark.

### How many should you make?

Start with one approved story for one page and one objection. Expand when you have a clear reason for the next asset.

### When should you expect results?

Set the evaluation approach before launch. Avoid promising a fixed return or deadline, and allow an inconclusive outcome.

## What should you do next?

Choose a review, apply PROOF, and test the presentation alongside the written version. Let the result determine your next production decision.
