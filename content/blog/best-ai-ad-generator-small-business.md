## How should you test an AI ad generator before buying?

Use my **Usable Ad Scorecard**. It evaluates four criteria: cost per usable ad, brand accuracy, formats, and learning curve. Apply it to a small, identical assignment before committing to a longer subscription.

Prepare a brief containing your website URL, approved product facts, brand references, intended audience, offer, and required outputs. Choose a realistic deadline. Keep those inputs consistent across tools.

Define an approved creative before you generate anything. My suggested standard is simple: you would be comfortable spending your own advertising budget on it after a final review.

### How do you measure cost per usable ad?

Use this formula:

**Cost per usable ad = (subscription cost + extra generation charges + editing-time cost) divided by approved distinct creatives.**

For a hypothetical comparison, value your editing time at an hourly rate you would actually pay. Record setup, corrections, regeneration, and export preparation. Keep those costs separate so you can see where the work went.

Count a resized version as another deliverable, but keep it separate from your count of original concepts. Otherwise, a collection of similar layouts can make your creative variety look larger than it is.

### How do you score brand accuracy?

Review the product depiction, logo, colors, tone, offer, and factual claims. Ask whether someone familiar with your business would recognize the result without seeing the tool that created it.

Use this proposed scale for every criterion:

- **0:** Fails the brief.
- **1:** Needs substantial rework.
- **2:** Becomes usable after meaningful edits.
- **3:** Needs only light corrections.
- **4:** Meets the brief after final review.

Reject any creative with an invented product benefit or inaccurate offer, regardless of its visual quality.

### How do you score formats and learning curve?

For formats, assess the complete deliverable: layout, readability, crop, motion, captions where needed, and export suitability for your planned campaign. Inspect the actual file.

For learning curve, record time to the first approved creative and whether you can repeat the process unaided. Give yourself the same time allowance in each tool.

Add the criterion scores for a total out of 16. Use that total as a decision aid, with factual accuracy as a mandatory pass. Do not assign scores to products you have not tested.

## Are AI ad generators worth it for small businesses?

They are worth considering when your own test demonstrates an acceptable cost per approved creative and a workflow you can repeat. Decide what improvement would justify the subscription before you begin.

For example, a local service business might prioritize accurate offer ads with minimal editing. A founder preparing a video campaign might prioritize script delivery and product depiction. Give those requirements different weights if they reflect your actual work.

Keep production value and campaign performance in separate columns. This comparison offers no measured CTR, ROAS, or sales-lift findings. Your buying test should establish whether you can produce suitable creatives; your campaign evaluation should address business results.

If your brief involves Meta Ads Manager, Facebook, Instagram, or Advantage+, write down your intended campaign and verify its requirements before approving files. For a creative-fatigue brief, ask for different messages and concepts, then assess whether the outputs meet that request.
