For Facebook ad sizes 2026, begin with a 4:5 feed image at 1440 × 1800 pixels.[^3] Adobe documents 9:16 Story templates at 1080 × 1920 pixels.[^2] Build separate feed and full-screen layouts, then check the actual ad preview before publishing.

## What are the Facebook and Instagram ad sizes for 2026?

Use this table to choose a starting canvas. Read the placement and media type together: an image recommendation should not automatically become your video specification.

| Placement or use | Aspect ratio | Documented export size | Source and scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook Feed, portrait image | 4:5 | 1440 × 1800 pixels | Buffer's single-image guidance.[^3] |
| Facebook Feed, square image | 1:1 | 1440 × 1440 pixels | Hootsuite's Facebook Feed image specifications.[^4] |
| Instagram Feed, portrait image | 4:5 | 1440 × 1800 pixels | Metadata's Instagram image guidance.[^1] |
| Instagram Stories, image | 9:16 | 1080 × 1920 pixels | Metadata's Instagram image guidance.[^1] |
| Facebook Stories, image or video | 9:16 | 1440 × 2560 pixels | Hootsuite's Facebook Stories specifications.[^4] |
| Facebook Reels, video | 9:16 | 1440 × 2560 pixels | Hootsuite's Facebook Reels video specifications.[^4] |
| Instagram Reels, video | 9:16 | 1440 × 2560 pixels | Metadata's Instagram video guidance.[^1] |
| Square Meta template | 1:1 | 1080 × 1080 pixels | Adobe GenStudio's supported template dimensions.[^2] |

These entries describe the cited publishers' guidance. They are not a complete list of accepted ratios, minimum dimensions, campaign restrictions or upload limits.

My recommendation for a small team is to organize production around **portrait feed, square and full-screen vertical**. Choose the relevant dimensions from the table, then make each composition readable on its own. Keep the same offer, product and visual identity across the set.

Do not treat an export preset as proof that the ad is ready. A correctly shaped file can still contain a headline that is too small or a product photo that is awkwardly cropped. Give the finished composition its own review.

## What size is a Facebook feed ad?

For a portrait Facebook Feed image, use the documented feed size in the table. Buffer also lists a square version, with JPG or PNG files and a maximum image file size of 30MB.[^3]

Start with portrait when your message needs a clear vertical sequence: headline, product, supporting detail and next step. For example, a hypothetical cleaning business could put its service promise above a photograph and its service area beneath it.

Choose square when the composition already works around a central object, such as a product pack or a simple offer. Do not add empty space solely to make that design taller. First decide whether the extra space would help explain the offer.

For either shape, review the final ad at a realistic viewing size. Ask someone to identify the business, the offer and the intended action without zooming in. If they hesitate, simplify the composition before changing dimensions again.

Buffer separates design recommendations from technical requirements, including minimum dimensions.[^3] Use the recommended export as your design target, and confirm the upload thresholds for your selected placement before launch.

## Should I use 4:5 or 1:1?

My starting recommendation is portrait for a feed-focused concept and square for a compact, centered composition. Treat that as a design decision to test, rather than a prediction about campaign results.

Adobe's template documentation describes portrait as providing more vertical space and square as a broadly compatible format.[^2] That supports preparing both, but it does not establish which will generate more leads for your business.

### When should you choose portrait?

Choose portrait when you want to stack the message naturally. A hypothetical skincare ad might have a short benefit statement, a product photograph and a small supporting detail.

Use the extra space deliberately. Increase the prominence of the product or improve the separation between elements. Avoid filling every available gap with another claim, badge or paragraph.

### When should you choose square?

Choose square when the main visual is already balanced and self-contained. A centered object, a short offer or a simple before-and-after concept may be easier to compose this way.

Before approving it, check whether any supporting detail has become too small. If the offer only makes sense after reading fine print, reconsider the hierarchy instead of squeezing the text.

### How should you compare them?

For a useful layout comparison, keep the headline, offer, product and destination consistent. Change the arrangement to suit each shape. Write down the decision you want to make before looking at results.

Avoid comparing a polished portrait ad with an unrelated square concept and calling the difference a ratio finding. That experiment changes the message and the shape together. Use it to compare complete concepts, or prepare a closer match if your question concerns layout.

## What size are Instagram story ads?

For Instagram Story ads, Metadata recommends 9:16 images at 1080 × 1920 pixels.[^1] Build the design on that vertical canvas from the beginning, then confirm the current requirements for your intended ad.

For a small business, I would begin with a single visual idea: one product, one service outcome or one clear offer. Decide what must be understood before arranging decorative elements.

Imagine adapting a hypothetical bakery offer. In the feed version, you might place the pastry photograph beside the headline. In the Story version, try placing the photograph below the headline, leaving generous space around essential copy.

Do not stretch the feed artwork to fill the new canvas. Return to the source photograph, adjust the crop and reposition the text. Compare the finished versions side by side to check that they communicate the same offer.

If you need a higher-resolution master, verify the appropriate recommendation for that exact placement. Matching the aspect ratio and matching the pixel dimensions are separate checks; record both in the brief.

## How should you size Reels and animated ads?

Use the video-specific rows in the table when planning Reels assets. For Instagram Reels, Metadata also advises keeping the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% on each side free of text and logos.[^1]

Treat those margins as a layout constraint for that cited use, rather than applying them indiscriminately to every Story, image and video format.

For an animated concept, review the whole sequence. Pause wherever the headline, logo, offer or next step appears. Check that each essential element remains readable throughout its appearance.

Try this review process:

- Inspect the opening frame without relying on motion to explain it.
- Pause on the main benefit or offer.
- Check any transition that moves important text.
- Inspect the closing frame and intended action.
- Watch again at ordinary playback speed.

If you adapt the same concept into a static ad, choose a frame or composition that can explain the offer independently. Do not assume the still image communicates an idea that depended on a sequence.

## How can you choose a ratio with the FRAME check?

Use my **FRAME check** before approving each export. This is an editorial checklist for production, not a Meta standard or a performance scoring model.

### F: Fit the intended placement

Write the placement and media type beside the filename. Match the chosen canvas to the relevant documentation. If you cannot name the destination, resolve that before exporting.

### R: Read the message without zooming

View the ad at a realistic size. Identify the first thing you notice and the next thing you read. Remove competing text if the hierarchy feels unclear.

### A: Anchor the essential elements

Mark the product, headline, logo and next step as essential. Keep them away from vulnerable edges and review them against the applicable placement guidance.

### M: Match the offer across versions

Compare the layouts together. Check the wording, eligibility conditions, product details and destination. A ratio adaptation should preserve the meaning of the offer.

### E: Examine the finished preview

Review the uploaded version before approval. Save a screenshot with the filename so another person can see which version you checked.

Give each item a pass or a revision note. Require every item to pass before launch. This keeps a subjective discussion such as “the vertical version feels wrong” focused on a specific fix: unreadable text, an awkward crop or a missing detail.

## How should you check your ads in Meta Ads Manager?

Buffer's setup guide shows creative previews in Meta Ads Manager and describes both Advantage+ placements and Manual placements.[^6] Use the preview review as a separate approval step after design review.

For each intended placement, inspect the exact asset you plan to publish. Check the headline, product, logo and next step. When reviewing motion, include the moments where essential information appears.

Keep your review notes concrete. “Move the offer away from the lower edge” gives a designer a clear task. “Make it pop” does not establish what needs to change or how to judge the revision.

Also record which settings and preview you reviewed. If you change the creative or placement choices afterward, repeat the affected part of the check. Do not rely on a screenshot of an earlier version.

Keep budget planning separate from the sizing decision. If you need help with the spending side, read [how much Facebook ads cost small business owners](/blog/facebook-ads-cost-small-business). Choose the layout for the message and placement, then evaluate it against your campaign's business goal.

## How can a small business build a reusable ad-size kit?

Prepare a short brief before creating variations. Include the offer, intended audience, essential product details, destination and required placements. Add a reference to the dimensions you selected.

Then prepare a layout set around the same concept:

- A portrait composition with a clear reading order.
- A square composition with a compact hierarchy.
- A vertical composition rebuilt for the full-screen use you intend.

Name files with the concept, shape and version. Keep the editable artwork with the exported files. When you revise the offer, update the brief first and check every variation against it.

Plan message variations separately from layout variations. For example, draft a problem-focused message and a product-focused message, then adapt each into the required shapes. That gives you a clear record of what changed when reviewing the work.
