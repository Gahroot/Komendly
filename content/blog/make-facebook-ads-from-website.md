To make Facebook ads from website content, choose a page with a clear offer, extract its benefits and visuals, and turn those ingredients into a focused ad. You can design it manually or use Komendly’s Ozzy to generate static and animated creatives from the URL. Then check the message against the destination page before publishing.

By Nolan Grout, founder of Komendly.

## Which website URL should you start with?

Start with the page you want someone to visit after clicking. For a specific service, choose its service page. For a product, choose the product page. Use your homepage when the goal is to introduce the business and its main offer is easy to find there.

Before creating anything, finish this sentence: “After seeing this ad, I want someone to…” Choose a concrete action: explore a product, request a quote or book a consultation.

For this walkthrough, imagine a fictional home-organizing business. Its kitchen service page explains what the service includes, shows real project photographs and invites visitors to request a consultation. Those are the ingredients for the ad. Treat every example below as illustrative copy, not a claim about a real business.

## What should I pull from my website into an ad?

Use my **PAGE checklist** to build a brief before you write or generate creative. It works for both manual production and a website to ads workflow.

| PAGE element | What to extract | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | The offer and main benefit | Does the destination page support the exact wording? |
| Audience | The customer’s situation or task | Does the opening address a recognizable need? |
| Grounds | Evidence supporting the message | Can you verify it and use it in advertising? |
| Execution | Visual, brand cues and next step | Do they belong to the same offer? |

For **Promise**, choose a specific benefit rather than copying a broad slogan. “Kitchen organizing with a professional organizer” gives the fictional business a more concrete starting point than “Transform your life.”

For **Audience**, note the problem the service addresses. In this example, that might be difficulty finding everyday kitchen items. Describe the situation without inventing research about customers.

For **Grounds**, collect relevant evidence: a real project photograph, an accurate service description or an approved customer quotation. Preserve qualifications. A review about one project should not become a promise about every project.

For **Execution**, gather your logo, brand colors, suitable photographs and the intended destination. Select an image that illustrates the offer rather than an unrelated decorative image.

## How do you adapt website copy to each awareness stage?

Use awareness stages as writing prompts. Keep the service consistent while changing what the opening explains. The following is a suggested message map for the fictional organizing business.

| Awareness stage | Question to write for | Example opening | Suggested visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | What situation might someone recognize? | “Where did the measuring cups go this time?” | A recognizable kitchen-search moment |
| Problem aware | What problem needs attention? | “Give everyday kitchen essentials a designated place.” | An organizer arranging a drawer |
| Solution aware | What approach could help? | “Explore professional kitchen organizing.” | The organizing process |
| Product aware | What does this particular service include? | “See what our kitchen organizing service includes.” | An actual project photograph |
| Most aware | What is the next step? | “Ready to discuss your kitchen? Request a consultation.” | A finished project and clear next step |

Use each opening only when the website supports the message. If the business does not offer consultations, replace that action with its actual next step.

For an initial batch, write one complete concept for each stage. Give each concept a headline, short supporting copy, visual direction and action. This creates a useful planning set without requiring you to run every concept immediately.

## How do you make the ads manually?

Use the PAGE brief as your working document and build one concept at a time.

1. **Write the opening.** Choose the awareness stage and answer its question in plain language.
2. **Add supporting copy.** Explain the relevant benefit or service detail. Remove sentences that introduce another offer.
3. **Choose the visual.** Use a photograph or illustration that supports this specific message.
4. **Build the layout.** Make the main message readable and keep essential details away from the edges.
5. **Check the destination.** Open the intended page and confirm that the offer and next step are easy to find.

For the solution-aware example, the draft might read: “Explore professional kitchen organizing. See what the service includes and request a consultation.” Pair it with an authentic process photograph, provided the business has permission to use it.

My recommendation is to choose manual production when you need precise control over the composition, wording or presentation of a particular image. Budget time for each revision and format you intend to prepare.

## What should you check before publishing?

Run a final PAGE check on the finished creative, including any animated version. Read the offer aloud, inspect the visuals and follow the destination link yourself.

Confirm that any conditions are still accurate. Remove unsupported superlatives, invented scarcity and guarantees your business does not offer. Check that a crop has not hidden essential wording or changed the meaning of the image.

The landing page deserves the same attention as the creative. Meta’s published advertising principles say its ad review considers the content, targeting, landing page and advertiser’s identity.[^2]

For AI-generated imagery, keep a record of the original assets and review how the finished image represents your product. In its June 2026 update, Meta said it would begin automatically detecting ads created or edited using third-party AI tools through industry-standard signals and would apply an AI info label in About this ad when detected.[^3] Treat that announcement as a plan, rather than confirmation that third-party detection has begun.
