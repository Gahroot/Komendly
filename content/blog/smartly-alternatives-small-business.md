## Do I need creative generation, campaign automation or both?

Start with the work that is blocked. Write the problem as a task: “We need approved ads for our next offer,” “Our designer needs precise layout control,” or “Our buyer needs campaign operations automated.” Those statements produce different shortlists.

For this evaluation, use four separate job definitions:

- **Generation:** producing the words and visual assets for an ad.
- **Design control:** correcting composition, typography, imagery and branding.
- **Publishing:** moving an approved creative into the intended advertising workflow.
- **Media automation:** executing campaign-management decisions, such as budget changes.

Do not accept a publishing demonstration as proof of budget automation. Ask the vendor to demonstrate the exact action you need, using an example from your own workflow.

If you use Meta Ads Manager, write down who will own campaign setup, Facebook and Instagram creative approval, budget decisions and reporting. Include any Advantage+ workflow you intend to use in that acceptance checklist. Treat those as requirements to verify, rather than assuming that an ad generator handles them.

For creative fatigue, specify the production response you want: a fresh hook, another awareness stage, a different visual concept or a revised offer. “Make more variations” is too vague to judge during a trial.

## How can I evaluate an alternative before paying?

Use the **GATE checklist: Generate, Adjust, Transfer, Execute**. It is a buying framework for this comparison, not a performance benchmark.

For every required task, mark one of three outcomes: demonstrated, needs a workaround, or unverified. Treat an unverified must-have as an unresolved purchase decision.

### Generate: does it produce the right message?

Give each tool the same offer, audience, website and approved brand assets. Request a problem-aware concept and a product-aware concept. Judge whether they communicate meaningfully different messages, rather than merely changing wording.

Use a written acceptance checklist: correct product, accurate offer, readable copy, suitable imagery and recognizable branding. Record time until approval, including review and corrections. A promising first draft is useful evidence, but your purchasing decision should concern the output you can actually approve.

### Adjust: can you fix what matters?

Request a realistic correction: shorten the headline, replace an image or correct the emphasis of the offer. Have the person who will own weekly production make the change.

Record whether they can complete it themselves, need a workaround or need specialist help. Include that work in your evaluation. Avoid selecting an editor based only on what a vendor demonstrates with a prepared example.

### Transfer: what happens after approval?

Follow an approved creative through your intended publishing process. Identify every remaining handoff, required account connection and plan restriction. Name the person responsible for the final check.

For Komendly, one-click Meta publishing belongs to Growth and Scale. If publishing is a purchase requirement, evaluate that plan requirement alongside creative production.

### Execute: which operational task is actually automated?

Ask for a demonstration of the specific campaign action you want delegated. Write down what triggers it, who approves it and how you would stop or reverse it.

If you only need creative production, mark this category optional. If media automation is mandatory, do not let attractive creative output substitute for evidence that the operational requirement is met.

## How should I compare the real cost of usable ads?

Use this planning formula: monthly subscription cost plus the value of production and review time, divided by approved creatives. This is a budgeting method, not a promised return.

Choose your own hourly value and count correction work honestly. Track rejected outputs too. Do not assume that a generation, a credit and a finished ad mean the same thing across vendors.

Before committing, confirm the billing period, renewal terms, cancellation process, trial limits and intended publishing entitlement. Prefer a short initial evaluation while those questions remain open.
