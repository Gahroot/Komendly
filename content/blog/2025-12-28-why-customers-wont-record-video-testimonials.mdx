Possible reasons customers decline video testimonials include camera discomfort, lack of time, uncertainty about what to say, or approval concerns. Start by asking what is getting in the way. Offer a written response or an audio conversation if recording a video does not suit them.

The practical question is how to capture a useful customer experience in a format that person willingly approves. Here is the approach I recommend, without assuming a refusal rate or promising a conversion lift.

## Why might customers feel uncomfortable recording video testimonials?

Treat camera anxiety as a possibility to ask about, rather than a diagnosis. Use a short reply menu:

- Would you prefer to write your answer?
- Would talking to an interviewer feel easier?
- Is the recording itself the obstacle?
- Do you need approval before appearing in marketing?

Preparation deserves attention too. Wistia's testimonial guide recommends sharing broad discussion topics rather than exact questions, and warns that pressure to deliver perfect marketing language can make answers unnatural.[^1]

My recommendation: explain the subject, intended audience, and proposed use before asking anyone to record. Make declining an acceptable response.

## What does an unfinished testimonial request actually cost?

Replace hypothetical lost-revenue calculations with an audit of your own process. Track requests sent, replies received, recordings submitted, approvals completed, and published assets.

Use the same definitions throughout. A customer agreeing to participate is different from a finished testimonial you have permission to publish.

Record the reason for each stalled request when the customer volunteers it. Leave unknown reasons marked unknown. Do not count silence as evidence of camera anxiety.

For a useful operational measure, divide approved assets by requests sent. Keep that collection measure separate from business outcomes such as purchases or qualified inquiries. A smoother collection process is worth testing, but it does not establish that video will outperform text.

## Why might traditional testimonial requests fall short?

### What should you change about the request?

Avoid starting with an open-ended assignment such as asking someone to describe everything they love about your business. Instead, invite them to explain the problem they wanted to solve, what they tried, and what happened.

Do not supply a positive result for them to repeat. The FTC says endorsements must accurately reflect the endorser's experience and opinion.[^2]

### When should you follow up?

Ask whether the timing works. If the customer prefers another date, agree on it. If they decline, close the request politely instead of treating repeated reminders as the default.

### What if a simpler recording link does not help?

Offer another format. My recommendation is to make writing, audio, and video equally acceptable at the collection stage. Choose the publication format after you know what the customer is comfortable sharing.

## What does the evidence support about collecting testimonials?

Wistia recommends treating a testimonial shoot as a conversation and allowing time for the interviewee to warm up.[^1] That is useful production guidance; it is not evidence of a universal acceptance rate.

For your own first test, choose a clear customer story rather than a target number of clips. Ask:

- Does it explain a recognizable problem?
- Does it describe what actually happened?
- Does it address a question prospects ask?
- Has the customer approved the intended use?

I would rather publish a specific, approved written account than stretch a vague compliment into a scripted video. Treat that as an editorial preference, not a measured performance finding.

## When is an AI-avatar alternative worth considering?

Komendly turns text reviews into AI-avatar videos. I am Nolan Grout, Komendly's founder, so that recommendation comes with a commercial interest.

Consider this route when you have a genuine written review, permission for the proposed adaptation, and a clear way to explain the presentation. Prefer a real customer recording when your goal is to show that person's delivery or demonstrate their own use of a product. Keep the review as text when a presenter adds little.

An avatar does not establish that the underlying experience happened. The FTC's rule has no blanket prohibition on AI avatars in marketing, but fake or false underlying testimonials are prohibited, and avatar use can still be deceptive under the FTC Act.[^3]

My recommendation is to label the presenter clearly and preserve the original review for reference.

## How can you use the CAMERA checklist?

Use my CAMERA checklist as a decision tool before choosing a recording method. It is a proposed workflow, not a validated scoring system.

### C: Confirm the customer's preference

Ask whether they prefer writing, audio, an interview, or recording independently. Do not infer that preference from their job title.

### A: Ask for a specific experience

Invite an account of the original problem, the purchase or service experience, and the outcome. Leave room for qualifications.

### M: Match the format

Use this decision table as a starting recommendation:

| Customer preference | Suggested route | Review before publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comfortable appearing on camera | Customer interview | Final edit and intended placement |
| Comfortable speaking but not appearing | Approved audio with supporting visuals | Audio, visuals, and attribution |
| Comfortable sharing only writing | Written quote or disclosed AI-avatar adaptation | Wording, attribution, and presentation |
| Unwilling to participate | Close the request | No publication from that request |

### E: Establish the permission scope

As a working practice, document the wording, name or anonymity preference, proposed format, and intended channels. Ask separately about paid advertising if that is a planned use.

### R: Retain the meaning

Keep the original and edited versions together. I recommend removing repetition only when the meaning remains intact, then sending the adaptation back for approval.

### A: Assess the result

Choose a placement hypothesis before publishing. For example, test whether an account addressing setup uncertainty helps visitors on a relevant product page. Compare against your current page and record the actual result.

## How should the approach change by industry?

These are suggested starting points, not industry benchmarks.

**Real estate:** Offer a written account after the transaction, with an optional interview later. Ask what details the client is comfortable making public.

**Restaurants:** Ask for feedback about a specific visit. If proposing a video adaptation, show the diner the intended wording and presentation before publication.

**SaaS and B2B:** Ask who should approve the quote, company name, and planned use. An avatar should not be treated as a shortcut around that approval.

**E-commerce:** Keep an account tied to the product and experience it describes. Do not turn praise for delivery into an endorsement of product performance.

## How can an AI-presented review stay transparent?

My recommendation is to explain the presentation in the video itself. Suggested wording is: “AI presenter reading an approved customer review; the presenter is not the customer.”

Use that wording only when it accurately describes your process. It is an editorial example, not a guarantee of legal compliance.

Check the full impression: presenter, narration, attribution, captions, and surrounding copy. Would a viewer think the synthetic presenter personally used the product? If so, revise the presentation before publishing.

## What are the most common questions about video testimonials?

### How many video testimonials should I start with?

Start with a customer story that addresses an important buying question. Add other accounts when they contribute a different experience or answer another objection. This is a planning recommendation, not a research-backed minimum.

### How long should a video testimonial be?

Use the shortest edit that preserves the customer's meaning and necessary context. Test length with your audience rather than treating a generic completion-rate claim as a rule.

### Can I turn a written review into an AI-avatar video?

Komendly turns text reviews into AI-avatar videos. My recommendation is to obtain approval for the adaptation and clearly explain that the presenter is synthetic.

### Does an AI-avatar disclosure make any testimonial acceptable?

No. The FTC says fake or false underlying testimonials are prohibited under its rule; avatar use can also be deceptive under the FTC Act.[^3]

### Will video testimonials increase conversions?

Treat that as a hypothesis to test. Compare a relevant video treatment with your existing page and measure a business outcome before deciding whether to expand it.

## What should you do when a customer still says no?

Accept the answer. Use the CAMERA checklist on your next request, and keep willing customers' preferred formats available.
