You can turn customer reviews into video testimonials without a camera by starting with genuine written feedback and using an AI presenter. Komendly turns text reviews into AI-avatar videos. My recommended process is to get permission for the planned use, preserve the customer's meaning, clearly identify the AI presentation, and ask the customer to approve the finished video.

I'm Nolan Grout, founder of Komendly, so this is a workflow involving my own product. The standard I recommend is straightforward: a viewer should understand whose experience is being described and who is presenting it.

Start with the review you can support, rather than the video you wish a customer had recorded.

## What is the real ROI question for video testimonials?

Before producing anything, choose the question you want the testimonial to answer. Will the service be dependable? Will onboarding be manageable? Does this product suit a business like mine?

My recommendation is to use customer feedback beside the buying concern it addresses. A review about communication belongs near information about service delivery. A review about implementation belongs near an explanation of getting started.

Treat that placement as a hypothesis to test. I would not build a budget around a promised conversion increase, production saving, or email performance improvement.

Write down the intended outcome before editing: a completed booking, a qualified inquiry, a purchase, or another action that matters to your business. Then compare the video with the written review already available.

For production planning, estimate the work you actually expect: permission requests, script review, generation, revisions, caption checks, and publishing. Include your team's time rather than comparing against an unspecified film-production budget.

## How can an AI-presented testimonial stay honest?

Keep the customer's experience separate from the presenter's identity. My recommendation is to explain that an AI presenter is reading approved customer feedback and avoid implying the avatar is the reviewer.

The FTC explains that consumer reviews featured in advertising or marketing become testimonials.[^1] Its guidance also says endorsements must reflect honest opinions and cannot make claims the advertiser could not legally make.[^2]

Use those principles as an editorial boundary. Don't turn praise for helpful support into a claim about revenue. Don't turn an account of one successful project into a promise about every project.

For labeling, consider plain language explaining that the video presents an approved customer review using an AI presenter. Treat that as an editorial starting point, rather than a guarantee that a particular presentation meets every applicable requirement.

If the review needs an invented backstory to make the video interesting, choose a different review.

## How do you transform reviews into video testimonials in four steps?

### 1. Select the review and request permission

Choose a review that describes an identifiable experience. Look for a particular problem, something the customer valued, or an explanation of why the product or service suited them.

Favor relevance over dramatic language. A practical account of clear communication may be more useful for your planned page than broad praise with little detail.

My permission checklist covers:

- The original review and proposed excerpt.
- The name, business, or other attribution you intend to display.
- The use of an AI presenter.
- The intended website, email, social, or advertising placements.
- Approval of the finished version before publication.

Ask for written confirmation and retain it with the source review. If you plan to use a customer photograph, logo, likeness, or voice, include that intended use explicitly in your approval process.

For feedback copied from a review platform, check the applicable reuse terms separately. Make permission and attribution part of the brief before generating anything.

### 2. Edit without creating a new customer story

Build the script around what the review actually contains. My preferred outline is the customer's concern, their experience, and the outcome they described. Leave a section out when the source does not support it.

Do not add a problem merely because a problem-solution story would sound stronger. Do not add a timeline, financial result, or purchasing motive that the customer never supplied.

Keep the original review beside the draft. For each script sentence, identify the supporting passage or ask the customer to confirm the additional statement in writing.

If you shorten a review, preserve qualifications that affect its meaning. If you paraphrase it, present it as an approved adaptation rather than an exact quotation.

For exceptional results, FTC guidance says advertisers without evidence that the experience is representative must communicate generally expected results.[^2] My practical recommendation is to leave impressive outcome claims out unless you can support their use in the advertisement.

### 3. Generate the video and review the presentation

Komendly's AI video testimonials turn text reviews into AI-avatar videos. That is the product capability this workflow uses.

After generation, review the complete asset rather than approving the script alone. Check pronunciation, wording, displayed attribution, any accompanying text, and the explanation of the AI presenter.

Add accurate captions in your editing workflow. W3C explains that captions include speech and the non-speech audio needed to understand the content.[^3] W3C also says automatic captions do not meet accessibility requirements unless confirmed fully accurate.[^3]

Check names, product terms, and any word whose omission would reverse the meaning. Review captions against the final audio, then send the complete video to the customer for approval.

### 4. Choose the placement before making more versions

Start with a specific placement and purpose. For a booking page, select feedback addressing the service experience. For an onboarding page, choose feedback about getting started.

For Facebook, Instagram, or an email campaign, decide which concern the planned message should address before shortening the script. Check the destination's current requirements before publishing.

Review every edit as its own asset. Keep the attribution, context, and AI explanation with the testimonial when you shorten or reformat it. Avoid approving a full version and assuming every excerpt communicates the same thing.

## How should you measure whether the video helps?

Use a test plan rather than a borrowed performance statistic. My starting point is the same approved customer feedback presented as text in one version and video in another.

Keep the offer, surrounding copy, and intended audience consistent where practical. Select the business outcome before starting, and record what changed between versions.

Track production effort alongside the outcome: time spent obtaining permission, reviewing the script, correcting the video, and preparing the placement. Use your actual work to judge whether the process fits your business.

My review sheet asks:

- Did the intended audience encounter the testimonial?
- Did the selected business outcome improve, decline, or remain uncertain?
- Was the video accurate and understandable?
- Did viewers raise questions about the presenter or attribution?
- Was the production effort reasonable for this placement?

Set a review point in advance. If the result remains uncertain, keep that uncertainty in the decision rather than calling the video a winner. If the text version performs better, keep using it and revisit the video only with a specific reason to change it.

## What should you check before publishing an AI testimonial?

I recommend the **CLEAR checklist**: Consent, Labeling, Evidence, Accuracy, and Relevance. Use it as an editorial review, not a legal certification or performance score.

| Check | Question to ask | Action if the answer is unclear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consent | Has the customer approved this wording, attribution, presentation, and planned use? | Request approval before publishing. |
| Labeling | Can a viewer understand that the presenter is AI? | Revise the explanation and presentation. |
| Evidence | Can you support the experience and outcome claims? | Remove unsupported claims or obtain appropriate support. |
| Accuracy | Do the script, audio, captions, and attribution preserve the source meaning? | Correct the asset and repeat approval. |
| Relevance | Does this review address a concern at this placement? | Choose a more relevant review or placement. |

Also review any relationship behind the endorsement. FTC guidance says unexpected connections that would affect how consumers evaluate an endorsement should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously.[^2]

Keep a publication record containing the original review, permission, approved script, final asset, and placements. When you revise the video, update that record rather than relying on approval of an earlier version.

## When should you use AI presentation versus a real customer recording?

My recommendation is to choose the format according to what you need the audience to understand.

Consider an AI-presented review when you have approved written feedback and want to test a narrated version. Keep the focus on the customer's account, with clear attribution and an explanation of the presenter.

Choose a real customer recording when seeing that particular person speak is central to your purpose. For an interview about a founder's decision, a detailed partnership story, or a recognizable customer's experience, I would favor the actual speaker when they are willing to participate.

Keep the written review when it communicates the point clearly and the video adds little. You do not need to turn every review into a video.

For an unresolved complaint, work on resolving the issue before considering a promotional adaptation. If a customer later wants to describe the resolution, request approval for that account instead of rewriting the original complaint into praise.

## What else should you know about video testimonials?

### How can I make video testimonials without filming customers?

Start with a genuine written review and request approval for its use. Komendly turns text reviews into AI-avatar videos. My recommendation is to identify the presenter as AI, preserve the review's meaning, and ask the customer to approve the finished video.

### How long should video testimonials be?

My recommendation is to cover one customer concern with the shortest complete account. Keep enough context to preserve the meaning, and test a shorter edit against a fuller version instead of assuming a universal ideal length.

### Does a public review mean I already have permission?

I would not treat public visibility as approval for the planned video. My workflow is to request written permission covering the wording, attribution, AI presentation, and distribution, then retain the customer's approval.

### Are AI-avatar testimonials automatically allowed?

No. The FTC says its rule does not categorically prohibit AI avatars, but their use can still be deceptive under the FTC Act.[^1] My recommendation is to use genuine feedback, identify the AI presenter clearly, and avoid suggesting the avatar is the customer.

### Do video testimonials convert better than written reviews?

I would not assume they will outperform written reviews for your business. Test the same approved feedback in text and video, keep other page elements consistent, and compare the business outcome you selected before the test.

### Where should I place a video testimonial?

My recommendation is to place it beside the concern it addresses: service reliability near booking, implementation feedback near onboarding information, or product experience near the relevant product. Keep the attribution and AI explanation visible.

## What is a practical first project?

Choose a genuine review, request permission, and prepare a narrowly focused script. Run it through CLEAR, obtain approval of the finished video, and test it beside the relevant buying concern. Use the outcome to decide whether another video deserves the effort.
